ZOOTOPIA CITY LOREBOOK I. GEOGRAPHY & DISTRICTS
* Savannah Central: The metropolitan heart of the city. It serves as the primary transport hub (Zootopia Central Station) and the center for commerce and government. Architecture here is scaled for all animals, featuring "small-scale" walkways and "large-scale" plazas.
* Sahara Square: A desert oasis known for luxury resorts and casinos. It utilizes advanced climate-control technology to maintain high temperatures and low humidity, catering primarily to camels, elephants, and other desert-dwelling mammals.
* Tundratown: A frozen wasteland turned urban center. It is characterized by perpetual snow and ice. The district uses massive cooling systems to maintain sub-zero temperatures.
* Rainforest District: A lush, tropical canopy city. Buildings are connected by high-altitude catwalks and bridges to avoid the damp, dense floor. It features an intricate irrigation system to maintain the humidity required by its inhabitants.
II. SOCIOLOGY & LAW
* The Mammal Inclusion Act: The foundational legal pillar of the city, stating that any mammal—regardless of species, size, or predatory nature—has the right to live and work in Zootopia.
* Predator/Prey Dynamics: While legally equal, a subtle social divide exists. Predators are often viewed with a subconscious fear (the 'savage' instinct), while prey animals make up the vast majority of the administrative and service workforce.
* ZPD (Zootopia Police Department): The primary law enforcement agency. Historically, the force has been dominated by larger mammals (rhinos, elephants, tigers), creating a glass ceiling for smaller species.
III. INFRASTRUCTURE & URBAN PLANNING
* Zoning & Scaling: Zootopia is divided into 'Scale Zones.' High-traffic areas feature 'Universal Access' architecture, while residential zones are often species-specific to optimize space. 'Small-scale' districts feature miniature plumbing and electricity, while 'Mega-scale' districts require reinforced foundations to support the weight of elephants and rhinos.
* The Transit System: The city relies on a sophisticated network of trains and buses. Specialized transport exists for different sizes, including 'Micro-Trams' for rodents and 'Heavy-Haul' carriages for larger mammals.
* Climate Control Systems: The 'Environmental Walls' are massive engineering feats. Tundratown is kept frozen via a subterranean network of liquid nitrogen cooling pipes, while Sahara Square utilizes giant geothermal heaters and humidity regulators to maintain a desert ecosystem.
IV. ECONOMY & INDUSTRY
* The Food Sector: A complex legal and ethical landscape. The city relies heavily on synthetic and plant-based proteins to avoid the conflict of predators eating prey. 'Bug-based' proteins are a staple in many mid-range eateries.
* Specialized Labor: Many industries are segmented by physical ability. For example, the 'Canopy Services' in the Rainforest District are dominated by primates and agile climbers, while heavy construction is primarily handled by bovine and elephantine labor forces.
V. SOCIAL DYNAMICS & ETIQUETTE
* The Predator Stigma: Despite legal equality, predators often face "instinctual" prejudice. This manifests in subtle ways, such as predatory mammals being discouraged from certain childcare roles or facing higher scrutiny in security checkpoints.
* Species-Specific Social Circles: While the city is integrated, organic "clusters" often form. Small mammals tend to congregate in the 'Micro-Zones,' while larger mammals frequent 'Grand-Plazas.' This isn't necessarily exclusionary, but it creates distinct cultural bubbles within the city.
* The 'Savage' Taboo: The concept of reverting to animal instincts is the greatest social fear in Zootopia. Maintaining a "civilized" demeanor is the highest social currency; any slip into primal behavior is viewed as a catastrophic failure of the social contract.
VI. LEGAL QUIRKS & CIVIL ADMINISTRATION
* Sizing Ordinances: The city has strict laws regarding 'Size-Appropriate' infrastructure. For example, it is a civil offense for a large mammal to obstruct a 'Micro-Walkway,' and conversely, small mammals are provided with 'Safety Elevators' to prevent accidents in high-traffic areas.
* The Dietary Accord: A set of guidelines ensuring that all food sold in public markets is "mammal-safe," meaning no meat products derived from mammals are permitted, ensuring the peace between predator and prey.
