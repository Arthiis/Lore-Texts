* Nick Wilde: A male fox, A master of the 'hustle' who hides a wounded heart. His wit is his primary defense mechanism against a world that decided who he was before he even grew up.
* Assistant Mayor Bellwether: A female sheep, Initially appearing as a timid, overlooked subordinate, she possesses a calculating mind and a deep-seated resentment toward those in power, fueling a dangerous ambition.
* Officer Clawhauser: a male cheetah, The heart of the ZPD front desk. Optimistic and food-loving, he serves as a social bridge and a source of morale within the high-stress environment of the precinct.
* Flash: A male sloth working at the DMV. He represents the ultimate bureaucratic hurdle, though he is fundamentally kind-hearted and diligent (at his own pace).
* Mr. Big: The godfather of Tundratown. Despite his diminutive size as a shrew, he commands absolute respect and loyalty through a complex network of favors and fears. A male mouse
* Mayor Leontis: The public face of Zootopia. He focuses on the image of harmony, often ignoring the simmering tensions beneath the surface to maintain political stability. A male Lion
* Yax: A laid-back yak who serves as a spiritual and emotional anchor for those in stress. He operates as a "zen master" of sorts, offering a perspective of calm and mindfulness in a city that is constantly rushing. His presence provides a necessary contrast to the high-tension environment of the ZPD and the Mayor's office.
* Finnick: A fennec fox with a gruff, cynical exterior and a surprising amount of tactical knowledge. Despite his size, he possesses a "big mammal" personality, often acting as the reluctant but essential partner in Nick's early hustles. He represents the struggle of small mammals trying to be taken seriously in a world built for giants.
* Manchas: A jaguar who serves as a cautionary tale within the city's social fabric. His story highlights the fragility of the peace between predators and prey, as well as the devastating impact of the 'savage' stigma on an individual's life and reputation.
* Gazelle: A female Gazelle, The city's premier pop icon. Beyond her music, she is a powerful cultural symbol of integration and harmony. Her influence is vast, and her public advocacy for mammal equality provides a hopeful counter-narrative to the fear-mongering of political opportunists.
* Gary D. Snake: A snake. He is a sophisticated and cunning antagonist who uses his poise and silver tongue to manipulate others and orchestrate schemes to alter the city's social order.
* Pawbert Lynxley: A lynx. He is a central figure in the second film's conflict, serving as a bridge between different species groups while navigating the city's evolving legal and social tensions.
* Nibbles Maplestick: A female beaver. Despite her small size, she is a spirited character who often finds herself in influential positions, using her perceived insignificance to gather intelligence and influence larger mammals.

Groups:

   The Transit Hub Operators: A diverse mix of mammals who manage the complex network of trains, buses, and ferries. This group includes the high-stress dispatchers, the patient ticket-takers, and the frantic commuters. They are the heartbeat of the city's movement and often possess the best "street-level" gossip.
   The Climate-Control Technicians: Specialized engineers who maintain the massive environmental systems of Tundratown and the Sahara Square cooling towers. They are often seen as a "blue-collar elite," possessing technical knowledge that keeps the city from collapsing into a chaotic mix of temperatures.
   The 'Scale-Zone' Architects: Urban planners and designers who specialize in "Multi-Species Integration." These professionals are obsessed with spatial validity, constantly arguing over the placement of miniature doors versus giant arches to ensure the city remains accessible to everyone from shrews to elephants.
   The Savannah Central Street-Vendors: A colorful array of entrepreneurs selling everything from organic insect protein to oversized umbrellas. They are the city's most observant residents, seeing every face that passes through the hub and acting as the unofficial historians of the city's daily ebb and flow.
* The 'Apex' Socialites: An exclusive, often exclusionary circle of high-society mammals. They frequent the most expensive lounges in Savannah Central and are obsessed with lineage, fashion, and political influence. Being part of this group is less about wealth and more about who knows you, creating a rigid social ladder that is difficult to climb.
* The Urban Naturalists: A passionate collective of mammals dedicated to preserving "wild" pockets within the city. They often clash with the 'Scale-Zone' architects, advocating for more unmanicured greenery and wildlife corridors. They are the type to organize grassroots protests against new luxury developments that threaten a local park.
* The 'Mixed-Species' Arts Collective: A bohemian group of poets, painters, and musicians who live in converted warehouses. They pride themselves on being "post-species," focusing on creative expression that transcends biological differences. Their gatherings are eclectic, loud, and often the birthplace of the city's newest cultural trends.
* The ZPD Alumni Network: A tight-knit, sometimes secretive group of retired officers. They maintain an unofficial information exchange, keeping tabs on the city's underworld and offering "off the record" advice to current officers. Their loyalty to one another is fierce, making them a powerful—if invisible—force in the city's legal landscape.
